How to use Antigone

An interactive guide to Sophocles' Antigone — text, semantics, video, and corpus statistics. Below is what each tab does and how to use it.

Information

The entry page. Background on the play, its structure, and how the corpus is organised.

What you'll find

  • Hero title — the name of the play and a short epigraph.
  • About the play — context, year, themes in a short prose summary.
  • Structure — a complete list of the 13 sections (Prologue → Exodos) and what happens in each.
  • Characters — every speaking role with a one-line description and the colour used for them across the app.
  • Themes — clickable tags that expand into short explanations.
  • Facts strip — quick numbers about the play (total lines, sections, characters).

How to use it

  • Use the Hero navigation below the title to jump to a section of the page.
  • Click any theme tag to see what it covers.
  • Scroll down — the back-to-top button appears in the bottom-right after you scroll past the hero.

Scenes + Lines

The reading workspace. Browse a scene in full with all translations side-by-side, then drill into any single line.

Components

  • Scenes bar — click any section (Prologue → Exodos) to load it.
  • Scene Reader — the full scene as a scrollable table. Columns: line number, speaker badge, Ancient Greek, Modern Greek, English, and optionally Chinese.
  • 中文 toggle — top-right of the Scene Reader. Shows or hides the Chinese column.
  • SPARQL Line Navigator — beneath the reader. Loads every annotated line from the Fuseki corpus and lets you step through them one at a time.
  • Three text panels — display the currently selected line in three languages.
  • Context sidebar — to the right of the panels. For the current line shows the speaker, the scene, the themes, the conflict, any myths referenced, and the speaker's motivations. All mined live from the RDF corpus.

How to use it

  • Click a scene → the Scene Reader fills with that scene's lines.
  • Click any row in the Scene Reader → the navigator jumps to that line, the three panels update, and the Context sidebar refreshes.
  • Use Previous Line / Next Line for sequential reading, or type a number and press Go.
  • Click 🔊 on a panel to hear the line read aloud.

Requires Fuseki running at localhost:3030/antigone.

Semantics / Video

Watch the play in sync with the semantic structure of each scene.

Components

  • Video player — full performance of the tragedy.
  • Scene tags — clickable tags that jump the video to the start of any section.
  • Three text panels — Ancient / Modern Greek / English previews of the current scene.
  • Semantic panels — themes, characters, events, and emotions tied to the current scene.

How to use it

  • Click a scene tag → the video jumps to that section's timestamp.
  • As the video plays, the surrounding panels update to reflect the current scene's context.
  • Click any character bubble to filter the view to scenes where they speak.

Statistics

Corpus-level analytics across the whole play.

Components

  • KPI cards (top) — five numbers: total RDF triples (live from Fuseki), sections, characters, distinct emotions, and total lines.
  • Emotion Heatmap — every section × every emotion. A coloured cell means that emotion is annotated in that section.
  • Knowledge Network — an interactive graph that grows from a clean overview into the full corpus, one category at a time. Spring-physics layout pulls connected nodes together.

The 5 zoom levels

The graph starts at Level 1 and you reveal more detail as you click through the levels:

  • Level 1 — Characters: just the 9 characters, connected when they share a scene. The clearest view.
  • Level 2 — + Emotions: adds the emotions each character carries.
  • Level 3 — + Themes: adds the themes alongside the emotions.
  • Level 4 — + Events: adds what actually happens — events linked to the people involved.
  • Level 5 — Full: adds the 13 scenes as central hubs. Everything in the corpus, with filter chips to focus on one category at a time.

How to use the Network

  • Level buttons — top row. Click any level (1 → 5) to switch view. The numbered badge shows the active level.
  • Drag any node with the mouse to rearrange the graph.
  • Hover a node — a tooltip shows its type, name, and number of connections; its edges light up in terracotta.
  • Filter chips — appear only on Level 5. Click All / Characters / Emotions / Themes / Events / Scenes to fade out everything except the chosen kind.
  • Fullscreen ⛶ — top-right of the controls. Toggles a borderless view that fills the whole window so you can explore comfortably. Press Esc to exit.
  • Reading the graph: larger nodes = more frequent in the corpus. Thicker edges = stronger co-occurrence. The colour of each node matches the legend below the graph.

Tip: start at Level 1 to see who interacts with whom, then step up to Level 2–3 to discover what they feel and stand for. Switch to Level 5 + filters when you want to inspect the full corpus.
